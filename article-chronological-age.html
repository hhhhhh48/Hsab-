Chronological Age vs. Biological Age

What's the difference and why it matters

Advertisement

You probably have one "age" in your mind, but science recognizes several types of age. The two most important are chronological age and biological age.

Chronological Age

Chronological age is the simplest: the exact amount of time that has passed since you were born. It is expressed in years, months, and days. This is the age on your birth certificate, passport, and medical records.

Why Chronological Age Matters

In education, chronological age determines school enrollment eligibility. In medicine, pediatricians use chronological age to assess a child's development against age-appropriate milestones. Speech therapists, psychologists, and educators all rely on it. For premature babies, doctors even calculate a "corrected age" by subtracting the weeks born early from chronological age.

Biological Age

Biological age (or physiological age) is how old your body appears based on biomarkers, fitness, and health. A 50-year-old marathon runner might have a biological age of 35, while a 30-year-old smoker might have a biological age of 40. Unlike chronological age, biological age can be improved through lifestyle changes.
